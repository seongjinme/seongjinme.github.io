## 0

I was thirteen, when my mother was my age. Back then, every adult around me seemed as solid as a mountain. I envied the weight of experience that my overgrown rebelliousness could not fathom. Now I know. Anxiety about the present and uncertainty about the future remain as pervasive as air, whatever your age. You merely become better at hiding your worries and acting strong.

I have worked all sorts of jobs. Packing dried seafood, working a supermarket fish counter, delivering national examination answer sheets, editing videos. My history is filled with full of experience that does not go on a resume. It was not because I had any particular taste for adventure. I had to do whatever I could to clear away the loans and payment demands piling up in front of me. In the two office careers that followed, I mistook being recognized for doing my work well for having found my calling. After years of treating company business as my own and working day and night, all that remained were a business closure, voluntary redundancy, and hospital bills.

Autumn 2020. Everything had come to a halt because of COVID. Only then did I have room to consider what I really wanted to do. I no longer wanted to remain someone who drew up other people's blueprints. I wanted to be able to make something worthwhile with my own hands, however small. Even if it meant resetting my entire career to zero.

I began doing all sorts of things. I found Harvard's CS50 lectures in English and transferred into Korea National Open University's Computer Science department. Barely able to read a line of code, I started trying my hand at projects. I kept going, thinking that if I walked steadily in one direction, I would eventually reach somewhere. I turned down every offer to return from people I had worked with. I did not want a way back. Sometimes, to reach what you want, you have to burn the bridges behind you.

## 291

I spent 291 days as a web frontend student at [Woowacourse](https://www.woowacourse.io). They were days full of precious relationships and valuable experiences.

I know how fortunate and privileged I was to spend my time studying something I loved at this age. Sometimes I even felt I had no right to it. But I did not want to let go of a hard-won opportunity. Spending ten months among a small group of talented people gathered from across the country is a chance that comes only a few times in a life.

![The final demo day at Woowacourse](/content/images/2024/12/woowacourse-demoday.jpg)

Combining learning and care was painful. My partner had to make considerable sacrifices so that I could spend eight hours a day on campus. Most of my time outside campus went into taking our dog to and from daycare and catching up on housework. At home, I had no energy left to read or write code. I even left my laptop in my locker. Late-night work, study groups, team dinners: I could not dream of them.

To study more efficiently within my limited capacity, I had to make the hours count. I devised my own strategy. My realistic goal was to improve enough that I would not simply be carried by my partner in pair programming. I picked out only the essential topics in the curriculum and practiced them. For things that seemed useful but not urgent, I skimmed the references and moved on. I took care not to disappear down rabbit holes over minor technical issues.

At some point, I simply let go of things like metacognition and retrospectives. Taking the dog to daycare, walking for two hours every day, and cleaning the house already left me exhausted. I did not want to spend energy on such higher-order activities as well. One good thing about getting older is feeling less guilty about skipping what everyone else does. You have to know how to give things up without making a burden of it.

![Yes, Dooboo, as long as you are happy... 😇](/content/images/2024/12/dooboo.jpg)

I made up for my lack of coding experience with the amount of information I took in. Like pouring water over a tray of bean sprouts, I read my peers' pull requests and reviewers' comments on each week's completed assignments during my commute. I did not memorize the details. I tried to develop a feel for approaches to solving problems and for readable code. It might look as though all the water simply drains out the bottom, but the sprouts do grow, however little. I took comfort in that.

I have no way of knowing whether this was the right learning method as Woowacourse intended it. It was the best I could do with the capacity I had. In any case, someone who did not even know what React was became able to implement something resembling a shopping cart as an SPA in four months. I decided that counted as a result.

![A review of my React shopping cart assignment at Woowacourse](/content/images/2024/12/woowacourse-review.png)

For our team project, I helped build [**Cruru, an applicant tracking system (ATS)**](https://www.cruru.kr). We aimed to simplify posting openings, recruiting, and evaluating applicants for inter-university clubs and small startups.

It was a successful project that lived up to its slogan, **"Complicated recruiting, sorted in a day with Cruru."** I was grateful for a mutually supportive way of collaborating that I had rarely experienced in my working life. Every teammate brought excellent expertise and teamwork to their own area. **My main role was to reduce the gray areas that code alone could not resolve.** I arranged interviews with people in the field, adjusted our product planning, built deployment automation, improved design and loading performance, and took the microphone on every demo day to speak for the team. So that everyone else could focus entirely on product features and quality.

![The dashboard for an individual opening in Cruru](/content/images/2024/12/cruru-dashboard.jpg)

The project taught me something. **Development is ultimately the sum of all the different tasks needed to solve a problem.** Time spent thinking through a problem together, conceiving and planning the product, finding and refining the form and design that best express that plan, and managing all those tasks through to completion. All of this belongs within the larger picture of development.

It also made me reconsider the relationship between users and code. Most customers are not curious about how code is written. They have no reason to be. What reaches them is only the usefulness and convenience of the product. Developer experience and maintainability matter, but the value a product gives its customers comes first. Since coming to think this way, I consider usability from the user's perspective whenever I build a page or component.

I believe a software engineer's role should extend beyond the narrow expertise of writing and fixing code. Remembering that **the essence is to benefit people through technology**, and **working toward a deeper understanding and more capable use of technology** to achieve that. **Respecting the expertise of the many disciplines involved in product development**, and **helping them work in harmony**. That is the direction I want to pursue as a software engineer. It is the lesson ten months of Woowacourse left me with.

## 1,555

September 21, 2020. I consulted a teacher I had met long ago in a small-group English class. This is what I wrote in my email:

> During a recent job search, I have become painfully aware of my limited English interview and business conversation skills. ... Speaking is so difficult. Whenever I try to speak English, it feels as though I am plucking words out of thin air, one by one, and assembling them. I struggle to start speaking before the sentence is complete, and end up unable to say what I wanted to say.

> I would like to practice speaking about three sentences smoothly at a time on any topic, in situations resembling interviews and work.

To leave my previous careers behind and start something new, I needed more room to move. I thought English might help. Without much of a plan, I started reading foreign news and taking conversation lessons. Twice a week, thirty minutes a day. I had no concrete plans for the future. If I could at least get myself speaking, perhaps opportunities to explore jobs or moving abroad would follow. I knew very little, but I was brave.

Then, on December 24, 2024, I confirmed that I would join Delivery Hero SE's Berlin headquarters as a Software Engineer. I would achieve my career change overseas. It had taken 1,555 days.

![So many emotions crossed in front of a single line of text](/content/images/2024/12/dh-offer.png)

Written this way, the years from 2020 to now might sound like a succession of carefully laid plans toward a grand dream. That could hardly be further from the truth. My life over those 1,555 days was muddled and indecisive. My career ambitions changed several times, as did what I studied.

At important crossroads, I made plenty of impulsive choices. Transferring to the open university was originally a third option squeezed between 42 Seoul and another college. One day I abruptly committed because "a four-year degree would be nice" and "the tuition is cheap." I threw myself into CKA certification with the absurd confidence that having used the cloud a little at my previous company meant I could quickly learn Docker and Kubernetes too. I originally applied to Woowacourse just to use the four-week precourse for self-study. Moving abroad was not even a possibility I was considering three or four months ago.

Many of the things I set in motion began this way, through luck, coincidence, and impulse. I never imagined that these chance events would converge into something that felt inevitable.

- **More than four years of English lessons** opened my job search far beyond national borders. I no longer had a reason to look for work only within this country.
- **The open university degree I earned after transferring three years ago** made me eligible for an EU Blue Card. It provides residence for up to four years and eligibility to apply for permanent residence after twenty-seven months.
- [**The news bot script I quickly put together to help my partner's work**](https://github.com/seongjinme/naver-news-fetching-bot) grew into a project used by more than a dozen companies and public institutions. It became a recurring topic in every interview during this hiring process.
- **Woowacourse, which I casually applied to for self-study and ended up devoting this entire year to**, gave me the skills and opportunity to start again as a software engineer abroad.
- **The CKA studies I abruptly began three years ago** gave me the background to build an RBAC-based CI/CD pipeline connecting AWS and GitHub Actions for our Woowacourse team project. That experience became key evidence of my proactive mindset and versatility in Delivery Hero's final interview.

Choices made at different times, for unrelated reasons, suddenly connected and opened a new path. Like scattered stars forming a constellation, shining as a compass in the night sky.

## 1

Next year, I will be in my first year again. As **a software engineer**, and as **an immigrant**.

Some people may see repeatedly circling back to a first year as inefficient. Especially those who believe every moment in life can be controlled, or who always pursue the path most advantageous to their résumé. But a human life cannot be such a simple straight line. We have to wind our way along crooked roads, bumpy roads, messy roads. Sometimes we take a wrong turn at an intersection or reach a dead end. We may have to retrace our steps. Sometimes we have to make a road that is not on the map.

This is an era without lifelong employers or lifelong professions. We have to live toward a hundred in a turbulent world, at home and abroad. There will be more first years ahead. I believe my many first years will become a source of flexibility and strength in overcoming difficulties amid upheaval, rather than a record of discontinuity.

Soon I will be far from all the people and places that have formed the basis of my everyday life. I have put our home on the market. For the first time, I will be far from the partner who has always been beside me for so long. I am not yet ready to accept a long separation from our dog, Dooboo. But I have no intention of being apart forever from the family who are everything to me. I decided to try life in Berlin first. As soon as I judge it a place where we could live together for the long term, I will bring everyone over. If it is not, I can look for a new path again.

I will always put first my wish to repay my beloved partner, family, and the people whose kindness made this new beginning possible. All my courage came from them. As always has been.
