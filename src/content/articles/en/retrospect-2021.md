## What I studied and learned

For the first time in ten years, I did nothing but study. Twenty years after first vaguely wanting to learn these things, I began. I thought I would be excited, but once I started, it was nothing extraordinary. I simply treated learning as my job. Out of a sense of professional duty, I put in at least forty hours of study every week. I did not miss a single week.

- Goorm EDU, C Programming Basics at a Glance (January-February)
- [Harvard CS50's Introduction to Computer Science 2021](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/2021/) (February-October)
- Transferred into the third year of Computer Science at KNOU and completed two semesters (March-present)
- Google Cloud Study Jam: BigQuery, Data Studio (March-April)
- AWS Builders Program: Docker, Container, DevOps (July-August)
- Docker/Kubernetes online bootcamp with Kakao Enterprise (August-present)

Everything I learned this year had been entirely absent from my life until last year. Little by little, parts of code and technical documentation that I thought I would never understand began to make sense. I also experienced making things I needed with my own hands.

- Built the [Powerzone HJ gym website](https://powerzonehj.com), added live reservations, and maintained the server
- Built a member check-in and management program for Powerzone HJ  
  (Google Apps Script, Google Sheets API)
- [Built a Naver News fetching bot](https://github.com/seongjinme/naver-news-fetching-bot)  
  (Google Apps Script, Google Sheets API)
- [Built and containerized a Flask-based blogging tool](https://github.com/seongjinme/tiny-blogger)  
  (CS50 Capstone Project / Python, JS, HTML, CSS, Docker)

One day, these results will look like the crooked handwriting on my childhood worksheets, back when I was just learning to take dictation. That is fine. Finding the courage to endure a clumsy beginning is enough. The Naver News fetching bot is a tool I desperately wanted seven years ago as an agency account executive, but gave up on because I did not know how to build it. Now I am someone who can make it myself.

## What exercise gave me

I also became a little more familiar with moving my body and using my strength. I experienced for myself the tired old saying that regular exercise changes your body. I lifted weights I had never lifted and pulled my body up in ways I never had. Taking each day as a chance to move forward just as much as I could brought stability to both body and mind. I owe that to a wonderful teacher in a wonderful place, [Powerzone HJ](https://powerzonehj.com).

Being able to do a pull-up does not make life dramatically easier. But now I know how it feels to lift myself above the bar. Making the impossible possible through steady practice gives me confidence in all sorts of things. Even if I fail this time, I can believe I will manage it next time.

## What I want to do next

I am preparing for my third career change. My goal is to become a cloud engineer.

At my previous company, I introduced [GCP](https://cloud.google.com/) and built an ETL pipeline for our app service data myself. Nobody at the company had cloud experience I could turn to, so I completed it alone. It was a considerable challenge for someone whose work had been business planning with Excel and PowerPoint. Looking back, though, it is the work I remember enjoying most. It made clear what I wanted to learn and work with next.

If things go as planned, I will be a new hire again in my late thirties. That is fine too. For now, I have put my anxiety about age aside. I kept walking forward, looking for work I could enjoy without burning out, work that would feel worthwhile. Looking back, the road simply turned out to be winding. Now that I have finally found where I belong, does it matter that I took a slightly longer way around? There can be no shortest path through a life.

## And what I realized

If I learned one thing this year, it is that belonging to a loose network of people who can openly give and receive affection and help is a healthier way to live than standing proudly alone, trying to establish my worth in isolation. That I could carry on with everyday life in a world of disease and unreason was entirely thanks to my partner and the people around me. I am simply grateful. All my courage this year came from them.
